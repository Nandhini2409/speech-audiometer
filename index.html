Speech Audiometer

A device that measures a person’s ability to hear and understand speech, supporting early detection and assessment of hearing loss.

Nandhini Settu · RA2311013010048
Department of Biomedical Engineering

About the Project

An audiometer identifies hearing loss and measures its degree and nature. Pure-tone tests show whether a person hears sounds, but a speech audiometer checks whether they can understand spoken words. It plays recorded or live speech at calibrated levels, supplies masking noise from a second channel, and is used to find the speech reception threshold and to assess hearing aids.

Objective

Measure the speech reception threshold (SRT) and speech discrimination score (SDS) accurately and repeatably.

Need

Tone tests alone do not show how well speech is understood, which matters most before prescribing hearing aids.

Outcome

A calibrated, two-channel speech test with level control, masking and separate ear testing.

Mechanism of Hearing

Sound waves are longitudinal pressure waves. The outer ear collects them, three middle-ear bones carry them to the oval window, and the fluid-filled cochlea turns them into nerve impulses for the brain.

Pinna & ear canal→Eardrum→Malleus · Incus · Stapes→Oval window→Cochlea (hair cells)→Auditory nerve→Auditory cortex

Outer ear

Pinna and ear canal lead sound to the tympanic membrane.

Middle ear

Three small bones couple the eardrum to the oval window. The Eustachian tube equalises pressure.

Inner ear

The cochlea converts fluid pressure waves into nerve impulses. High frequencies act at the base, low at the apex.

Types and Degrees of Hearing Loss

Conductive loss is reduced transmission of sound through the middle ear. Sensori-neural loss is a disorder of the cochlea and its nerve connections. The audiogram separates the two and shows the degree of loss in dB HL.

Block Diagram of Speech Audiometer

Speech from a live microphone, audio player or CD is chosen by the selector, gated by the interrupter, set to the required level by the attenuator, and sent to the chosen ear through the switching network.

Components and Their Function

Microphone + two-stage amplifierLive-voice input, mainly for communication with the patient
Tape / audio player, CDRecorded word lists for threshold and discrimination tests; also recorded masking signals
SelectorSelects which source feeds the audiometer
InterrupterSwitches the signal on and off
AttenuatorSets the output level, in 5 dB steps
VU meterShows the speech level so it can be kept calibrated
Noise generator (second channel)Supplies masking noise to the non-test ear
Switching networkRoutes the signal to the left or right earphone
Headphones or loudspeakersOutput transducers; the two speakers are 25 W each

Speech Audiometry Tests

SRT

Speech Reception Threshold: the level at which the patient correctly repeats 50% of two-syllable words (spondees) such as shotgun, aeroplane, workshop.

SDS

Speech Discrimination Score: the number of words correctly repeated, expressed as a percentage.

Test Procedure

1. Place earphones→2. Test one ear at a time→3. Play spondee word list→4. Patient repeats each word→5. Lower the level in steps→6. Record the 50% level

Test Environment

Sound cabin

Live-voice tests are done in a sound cabin so the patient cannot hear the audiologist directly. Same-room testing gives false results, especially with near-normal hearing.

Two-room set-up

The audiologist works in the equipment room with the speech audiometer while the patient is tested in the evaluation room.

Attenuation

Double-wall construction with absorbing material gives about 25 dB attenuation at 125 Hz and 60 dB between 1000 and 8000 Hz.

Live vs recorded

Live voice is mainly for communication because it is unreliable. Recorded speech gives repeatable results.

Applications

  • Finding the speech reception threshold for diagnosis
  • Assessing hearing-aid performance and selecting aids
  • Hospital and ENT clinic hearing assessment
  • Follow-up monitoring and rehabilitation
